Appendix B: FAQ and common mistakes

The questions people ask most in their first weeks, then every "Common mistakes" box of the pack in one place.

Questions

I cannot plan an audit from a program. Why?

Only the leader of the program's team can plan it. Check the team on the program and its leader in Team List. See chapters 07 and 08.

I expected a task and it is not there.

Look in To Do, not only in your email. The first task of a planned audit arrives on its planned start date, not when the audit is created. An evidence round starts on its own start date. See chapter 05.

The finding lists on the sign-off are empty.

Finding(s) for CAPA offers only major findings, and each finding can be handed over once. If the lists are still empty, open the CAPA or quality event by hand with New CAPA or New Quality Event and link the audit. See chapter 11.

Can one finding go to a CAPA and a quality event?

No. A finding is handed over once. If a quality event later turns out to need a CAPA, the quality event moves on to one.

What does RIV-CAPA-AFI-00193 mean?

RIV is the site code, CAPA the record type, AFI the source, an internal audit finding, and 00193 the running number. See chapter 12.

My CAPA has no effectiveness plan.

A CAPA creates one only when its Frequency under Effectiveness Monitoring is set. See chapter 12.

Why do my CAPA action items say "Action Items (No CAPA)"?

That is the name of the process that runs every action item, wherever it comes from. The name is misleading; the action item still belongs to the CAPA.

The effectiveness check failed. Do I open a new CAPA?

No. In Check Effectiveness And Approve, mark the actions to redo and pick Execute Action(s). They go out again within the same CAPA. If the root cause was wrong, say so in the notes. See chapters 03 and 12.

Who can see the audit menus?

Everyone. Roles decide who gets a task, not who can open a form. See chapter 15.

Common mistakes, by chapter

01 Why companies audit themselves

  • Treating the audit as a test that people pass or fail. People then hide problems, and the audit finds nothing useful.
  • Letting people audit their own area. They see what they expect to see.
  • Auditing only before the certification visit. A yearly rush finds less than a steady plan.

02 How an internal audit works

  • Writing a finding without evidence. "Some gauges look old" cannot be checked. "Gauge G-201, due 1 August, used on 14 October" can.
  • Grading every finding as major. When everything is urgent, nothing is.
  • Closing the audit with findings nobody owns. Every finding that needs work goes to a CAPA or a quality event before sign-off.

03 From finding to fix: CAPA

  • Stopping at the correction. The gauges get recalibrated, and the cause stays.
  • Naming a person as the root cause. "Operator error" leads to retraining one person, and the next person makes the same mistake.
  • Closing the CAPA when the actions are done, without an effectiveness check.
  • Opening a new CAPA when the check fails. Reopen the root cause analysis of the same one instead.

04 Not every finding needs a CAPA

  • Sending every finding to a CAPA. The CAPA team drowns in small slips, and the serious problems wait.
  • Fixing a slip and never looking again. Two slips of the same kind are a pattern, and a pattern needs a CAPA.
  • Ignoring observations because they need no action. They are the cheapest problems to fix, before they become findings.

05 Finding your way

  • Waiting for an email instead of checking To Do. The task is there even when the email was filtered as spam.
  • Closing the panel without Save. Unsaved entries are lost.
  • Picking a Task Resolution and forgetting Submit. The task stays open, and the next person never gets it.

06 Requirements and tests

  • Writing a requirement nobody can check, such as "Gauges are well maintained". Write what an auditor can see or count.
  • Copying the same rule twice under different names. Search the list before you add.
  • Adding a test to every requirement. A test is for proof over time. A label on a gauge needs one look, not a sample.

07 Audit programs

  • Picking the wrong team. The team's leader approves the program and is the only one who can plan it.
  • Writing a new program each year. Copy last year's and change what changed.
  • Approving a program without reading the requirements. A wrong requirement in the program means a wasted audit.

08 Planning and scheduling an audit

  • Planning the audit as someone other than the team leader. The product refuses to plan it.
  • A planned start date before the program's start of period. The product rejects the dates.
  • Leaving every requirement with the lead auditor. Share them, or one person does the whole audit.
  • Forgetting the auditees. They get no task, so the emails are all they see.

09 Collecting evidence

  • Attaching evidence from the wrong period. Check the round dates in the task subject first.
  • Approving evidence without opening it. The auditor relies on it as checked.
  • Leaving round 2 for later and forgetting it. Each round has its own task and its own dates.

10 Running the audit and recording findings

  • Closing the requirement panel and the task without Save on the task. The findings are lost.
  • Notes without facts. "Some gauges are overdue" is not a finding. Name the gauges and the dates.
  • Grading by feeling. Use the rules in chapter 02. Systemic or reaching a customer is major, one slip is minor.
  • Submitting the lead auditor's task before the other auditors are done. The product warns that their tasks are still open.

11 Signing off and handing findings over

  • Signing off before handing the findings over. Check the finding lists first.
  • Picking several findings at once when each needs its own owner. They end up in one record.
  • Forgetting the reviewer. Without QE / CAPA Reviewer, nothing can be created.

12 Running a CAPA

  • Approving a plan with only a correction in it. The cause stays, and the problem comes back.
  • Filling in the effectiveness check on the day the actions finish. Give the fix time to show.
  • Leaving the effectiveness notes empty. The person closing the CAPA has nothing to judge.
  • Leaving Frequency empty when the check must repeat. Without it, no effectiveness plan is created.

13 Action items, and the quality event route

  • Clicking Action Complete without Enter your findings or a Resolution. The product refuses the task.
  • Marking a temporary fix as Permanent. The effectiveness check then looks at the wrong thing.
  • Signing off an action without opening the evidence.
  • Sending a pattern of slips through quality events one by one. The same slip twice needs a CAPA.

14 Keeping track: lists and dashboards

  • Reading a dashboard as the state of one audit. The charts count everything in the system.
  • Looking only at My Action List. It shows your own actions, not your team's.
  • Treating "completed" as "effective". An action can be done and still not fix the problem. The CAPA's effectiveness check answers that.

15 Setting up Audit and CAPA

  • Going live with empty lists. Users find empty dropdowns and type workarounds into text fields.
  • A site without a code. The CAPA ID then has no site prefix.
  • No Director of Quality role. Every action item needs a reviewer picked by hand.
  • A team without members. The audit owner cannot pick any auditors.

16 Connection to the process repository

  • Importing a whole folder when only a few rules apply. The requirement list fills with items nobody audits.
  • Forgetting Sync by Module after the repository changes. The requirements keep the old wording.
  • Expecting archived results without setting the EPC Settings folders.
